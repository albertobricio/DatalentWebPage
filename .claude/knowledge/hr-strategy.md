# Knowledge Domain: HR Strategy

## 1. Core Concepts

- **HR as strategic partner, not administrative function.** Effective HR strategy is explicitly linked to business strategy — it answers "what workforce capability does our strategy require?" not just "how do we run HR processes well?"
- **Operating model design.** How HR organizes itself (business partners embedded in units, centers of excellence for specialized expertise, shared services for transactional work) shapes what strategic work is actually possible.
- **Talent segmentation.** Not all roles matter equally to strategy execution. Identifying *pivotal roles* — the small subset whose performance variance disproportionately affects business outcomes — is a prerequisite for focused strategic workforce investment.
- **Culture as a designed system, not a stated value.** Culture is the product of what an organization actually measures, rewards, and tolerates — not what its values poster says.
- **The employee lifecycle as a strategic system.** Attraction, selection, onboarding, development, retention, and offboarding are interdependent; optimizing one stage while ignoring others (e.g., aggressive hiring with weak onboarding) undermines the whole.

## 2. Terminology

- **Employee Value Proposition (EVP)** — the distinct set of benefits (financial and non-financial) an employee receives in exchange for their contribution; the "why work here" answer.
- **HR Business Partner (HRBP)** — an HR role embedded with business leadership, translating business strategy into workforce strategy.
- **Center of Excellence (CoE)** — a specialized HR team (e.g., comp, talent acquisition, L&D) providing deep expertise across the organization rather than duplicated in each unit.
- **Operating model** — the structural design of how HR delivers its services (HRBP / CoE / Shared Services being the most common three-legged-stool model).
- **Pivotal role** — a role whose performance variation has outsized impact on strategic outcomes, as distinct from roles that are merely hard to fill.
- **Succession bench strength** — the number and readiness of internal candidates prepared to step into critical roles.
- **Internal mobility** — movement of employees between roles within the organization, as an alternative to external hiring.
- **Employee Experience (EX)** — the sum of an employee's perceptions and reactions to their work environment, treated as a design discipline analogous to customer experience.

## 3. Best Practices

- Build an explicit strategy map connecting business objectives → required capabilities → workforce plan → HR initiatives, so every HR investment traces back to a business reason.
- Identify pivotal roles deliberately rather than treating all hard-to-fill roles as equally strategic.
- Build succession depth specifically for pivotal and critical roles, not a blanket program applied evenly everywhere.
- Measure culture through behavioral and outcome data (retention of high performers, internal mobility patterns, engagement survey driver analysis), not just perception surveys alone.
- Involve HR leadership in the strategic planning cycle itself, not only in translating an already-finalized strategy into HR programs after the fact.
- Design the EVP around what's genuinely differentiated and defensible, not generic claims every competitor also makes.

## 4. Frameworks

- **Ulrich HR Business Partner Model** — four roles: Strategic Partner, Change Agent, Employee Champion, Administrative Expert. A mature HR function operates across all four, not just the administrative one.
- **McKinsey 7S Framework applied to talent** — Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Skills, Style, Staff; used to check whether workforce capability (Skills/Staff) is actually aligned with stated Strategy.
- **9-Box Grid (performance × potential)** — used for succession planning and talent segmentation, plotting individuals on current performance against future potential.
- **Balanced Scorecard applied to HR** — translating HR strategy into Financial, Customer, Internal Process, and Learning & Growth metrics, avoiding a purely activity-based HR scorecard.
- **SHRM Competency Model / CIPD Profession Map** — competency frameworks defining what strategic HR capability actually looks like at the practitioner level.
- **Bersin HR Capability Maturity Model** — stages from reactive/administrative HR through to predictive, business-integrated HR.

## 5. Decision Trees

**Centralize or decentralize the HR function?**
- Does the business unit have distinct enough talent needs and scale to justify dedicated HR capacity? If yes and the unit is large/distinct → embed a dedicated HRBP.
- Is the capability highly specialized and best delivered consistently across the org (e.g., comp design, HRIS)? → Centralize as a Center of Excellence.
- Is the work transactional and high-volume with low strategic differentiation (e.g., payroll processing)? → Shared services / outsource.

**Build, buy, or borrow talent for a capability gap?**
- Is the skill available in the internal talent pool with reasonable development time? → Build (internal mobility/reskilling).
- Is the skill scarce externally and mission-critical long-term? → Buy (external hire), accepting higher cost and time-to-productivity.
- Is the need temporary or highly specialized for a short period? → Borrow (contractor/interim) rather than a permanent hire.

## 6. Anti-Patterns

- HR strategy as an annual static document, disconnected from ongoing business planning cycles.
- Treating all roles as equally strategic, diluting investment in the pivotal few.
- Stated culture values with no connection to actual incentive, promotion, or reward systems — the classic "say-do gap."
- Succession planning performed once a year in a single meeting, then shelved until the next cycle.
- An EVP built from generic claims ("great culture," "work-life balance") indistinguishable from every competitor's EVP.

## 7. KPIs

- Revenue (or value-add) per employee
- HR cost per employee / cost-to-serve
- Strategic workforce plan coverage (% of pivotal roles with an active plan)
- Succession bench strength (ready-now candidates per critical role)
- Internal mobility rate
- EVP awareness and perception score among target talent segments
- Leadership pipeline health (ratio of ready successors to critical leadership roles)

## 8. Common Mistakes

- Producing an HR strategy document without first identifying pivotal roles, resulting in evenly-spread rather than focused investment.
- Allowing stated culture and actual reward systems to diverge without noticing or correcting it.
- Treating succession planning as a compliance exercise rather than an active talent-development driver.
- Setting workforce plans without connecting them to the financial/business plan, producing HR initiatives the business can't actually fund or use.
- Copying a competitor's EVP language rather than identifying what's genuinely distinct about the organization's own offer.

## 9. References to Standards

- **ISO 30414** — Human Capital Reporting guidelines, covering workforce cost, diversity, leadership, organizational culture, and succession planning metrics.
- **SHRM Body of Competency and Knowledge (SHRM-BoCK)** — competency standard for HR professionals.
- **CIPD Profession Map** — UK-originated but widely referenced professional standard for HR practice.
- **ISO 10667** — standard for assessment service delivery in work and organizational settings.
- **GRI 401/402/405** — Global Reporting Initiative standards for labor practices and diversity disclosure, increasingly relevant as ESG reporting requirements expand.
- **World Economic Forum Future of Jobs Report** — widely cited methodology and dataset for workforce/skills trend analysis.

## 10. Future Trends

- Shift from job-based to skills-based organization design, changing how pivotal-role and succession analysis is done.
- AI-augmented strategic workforce planning, enabling more frequent re-forecasting than the traditional annual cycle.
- ESG-linked HR strategy, with human capital metrics (per ISO 30414 and GRI standards) increasingly treated as material disclosures, not internal-only metrics.
- Internal talent marketplaces becoming a standard mechanism for build-vs-buy decisions, blurring the line between succession planning and day-to-day mobility.
- Continued rise of flexible/hybrid work strategy as a core EVP and org-design variable, not a peripheral policy question.

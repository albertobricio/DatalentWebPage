# Knowledge Domain: Marketing

This file contains knowledge *about* marketing — frameworks, terminology, and standards a marketing practitioner would know — for agents to reason with. It is reference material, not marketing copy, and produces none on its own.

## 1. Core Concepts

- **Positioning precedes messaging.** Positioning defines the competitive category and comparison set a company competes in; messaging is how that position is communicated. Writing messaging before positioning is settled produces confident-sounding copy built on an undefined foundation.
- **Category creation vs. category entry is a deliberate strategic choice, not a default.** Claiming a new category (e.g., "the first X for Y") is powerful but only credible with genuine evidence no existing category fits; otherwise, entering an existing category with sharper differentiation is the safer, more defensible path.
- **An Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) disciplines everything downstream.** Content, campaigns, and even product prioritization drift without a documented, specific ICP to qualify against.
- **Demand generation and lead generation are different disciplines.** Demand generation builds awareness and preference before a buyer is in-market; lead generation captures buyers already showing intent. Conflating the two leads to premature hard-sell messaging aimed at an audience that isn't ready for it.
- **Category claims require third-party validation to be credible.** A company calling itself a "leader" in a category is marketing; an analyst, press outlet, or independent customer validating that claim is evidence. The distinction matters enormously to a sophisticated B2B buyer.

## 2. Terminology

- **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** — a specific, documented description of the organization type most likely to buy and succeed with a given offering (industry, size, maturity, pain signals) — distinct from a buyer persona, which describes an individual role.
- **Positioning** — the category and competitive comparison set a company claims to compete in, and why it wins there.
- **Messaging framework** — the structured articulation of positioning into audience-specific language and proof points.
- **MQL / SQL** — Marketing Qualified Lead / Sales Qualified Lead — the handoff points in a funnel, requiring a shared, documented definition between marketing and sales to avoid lead-quality disputes.
- **ABM (Account-Based Marketing)** — a go-to-market motion targeting a defined list of specific accounts with tailored campaigns, as opposed to broad, undifferentiated lead generation — typically used for enterprise motions with a small number of high-value target accounts.
- **Category creation** — establishing and naming a new market category a company can credibly claim to lead, as opposed to competing within an already-defined category.
- **Share of voice** — a company's relative visibility (media mentions, search presence, analyst coverage) compared to named competitors.
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** — the fully loaded cost of acquiring a customer, used alongside CAC payback period to assess go-to-market efficiency.
- **Content funnel (TOFU / MOFU / BOFU)** — Top/Middle/Bottom of Funnel, describing content suited to awareness, consideration, and decision stages respectively.
- **Analyst relations (AR)** — the discipline of engaging industry analysts (e.g., Gartner, Forrester) on a sustained basis, distinct from a single briefing.

## 3. Best Practices

- Settle positioning (category, comparison set, differentiation) before drafting messaging or campaign content.
- Document the ICP explicitly and qualify content, campaigns, and even sales outreach against it, rather than pursuing broad appeal.
- Treat analyst relations as a sustained relationship-building discipline (regular briefings, inclusion in research cycles), not a one-time outreach.
- Measure content and campaigns by downstream pipeline influence, not just traffic or engagement vanity metrics.
- Align marketing and sales on a single, documented MQL/SQL definition before scaling spend, to avoid downstream lead-quality disputes.
- Maintain positioning consistency across every channel (website, sales deck, proposals, analyst briefings) — inconsistent positioning across touchpoints undermines credibility faster than weak positioning stated consistently.

## 4. Frameworks

- **April Dunford's Positioning Method ("Obviously Awesome")** — a five-step method: identify true competitive alternatives → identify unique attributes → map attributes to value → identify who cares most about that value → determine the market category that makes the value obvious. A widely used, practical B2B positioning methodology.
- **The 6 Building Blocks of Positioning** (Dunford) — competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, target market characteristics, market category, and relevant trends — the components a complete positioning statement must address.
- **StoryBrand framework** — frames the customer as the hero of the narrative and the brand as the guide providing a plan and calling them to action, useful for message clarity in customer-facing content.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)** — analyzing what "job" a customer is really "hiring" a product/service to do, shifting focus from product features to the underlying customer outcome sought.
- **TOFU/MOFU/BOFU content funnel** — structuring content strategy by funnel stage rather than producing undifferentiated content regardless of buyer readiness.
- **RACE framework (Reach, Act, Convert, Engage)** — a structured planning model for digital marketing across the full customer lifecycle, not just acquisition.

## 5. Decision Trees

**Category creation or category entry?**
- Is there a genuinely new problem-solution fit with no existing category that fairly describes it, and does the company have the resources to sustain category-education marketing over time? → Category creation may be justified.
- Does an existing category reasonably describe the offering, with room to win through sharper, evidenced differentiation within it? → Category entry with strong positioning is the lower-risk, more common correct choice.

**Brand-awareness play or demand-generation play?**
- Is the target buyer segment largely unaware the problem/solution category exists yet? → Brand-awareness/education content (TOFU), not a hard conversion push.
- Is the target buyer segment already aware and actively evaluating solutions? → Demand/lead-generation content (MOFU/BOFU) with clear conversion paths.

## 6. Anti-Patterns

- **Messaging before positioning** — producing confident-sounding copy without a settled category and comparison set behind it, resulting in generic, undifferentiated claims.
- **Unvalidated category leadership claims** — asserting "leader in X" with no independent evidence (analyst coverage, press validation, customer proof) a sophisticated buyer or analyst would actually accept.
- **Vanity-metric-driven content strategy** — optimizing for traffic or engagement without tracking pipeline influence, making it impossible to know if content marketing is actually working.
- **Marketing-sales handoff misalignment** — no shared MQL/SQL definition, causing chronic disputes over lead quality and undermining trust between the two functions.
- **Positioning drift** — allowing positioning to change unintentionally across different pieces of content over time, with no version-controlled source of truth for what the company currently claims and why.

## 7. KPIs

- MQL-to-SQL conversion rate
- Pipeline influenced/sourced by marketing
- Content-to-opportunity conversion rate
- Share of voice relative to named competitors
- Analyst relationship maturity (briefing cadence, inclusion in published research)
- Branded search volume trend (a proxy for awareness/category association)
- CAC and CAC payback period

## 8. Common Mistakes

- Asserting category leadership with no independent evidence — a claim any competent analyst, journalist, or sophisticated buyer will immediately test and, if it fails, will discount everything else the company says.
- Under-investing in analyst relations while directly competing against analyst-covered incumbents, ceding an entire evaluation channel by default.
- Treating thought leadership as a series of one-off content pieces rather than a sustained, cited research program that compounds credibility over time.
- Allowing marketing and sales to operate on different definitions of a qualified lead, producing avoidable friction and pipeline distortion.
- Letting positioning drift silently across channels without a single, version-controlled source of truth for the current claim and its evidence.

## 9. References to Standards

- **April Dunford's positioning methodology** — the widely adopted industry-standard reference for B2B positioning practice (not a formal standards body, but the de facto reference).
- **Gartner / Forrester analyst engagement models** — the established practice models for structured analyst relations in B2B technology marketing.
- **Content Marketing Institute (CMI) benchmarking research** — widely cited industry benchmarking data for content marketing practice and measurement.
- **IAB (Interactive Advertising Bureau)** — digital marketing measurement standards.
- **GDPR / ePrivacy Directive** — directly constrain marketing data collection, tracking, and consent practices for any EU-facing marketing activity, including cookie-consent mechanisms.

## 10. Future Trends

- AI-generated content increasing content volume industry-wide, pushing genuine differentiation toward proprietary data and original research rather than volume or polish alone.
- Growing analyst attention specifically toward "agentic AI" as its own distinct coverage category, changing how AI-adjacent vendors need to engage analyst relations.
- Rise of answer-engine optimization (AEO) — optimizing for visibility within AI-generated answers/summaries, alongside (not replacing) traditional SEO.
- Continued shift toward privacy-first marketing (reduced third-party data reliance, consent-driven personalization) as GDPR/ePrivacy enforcement and browser-level privacy changes accelerate.

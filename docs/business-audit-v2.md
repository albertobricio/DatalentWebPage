# Business Audit v2 — Executive Panel Review

**Scope:** Business value only. No code, no CSS, no framework considerations. This audit evaluates the three live pages (Home `/`, Newsletter `/newsletter`, FAQ `/faq`) purely as sales, positioning, and market-credibility assets.

**Method:** Each page is reviewed through six buyer/analyst lenses, then rewritten. The six personas:

| Persona | What they actually care about |
|---|---|
| **CHRO** | Board-level narrative, risk, retention/productivity outcomes, reference-ability |
| **Compensation Director** | Pay equity, benchmarking, comp/total-rewards coverage, regulatory timing (EU Pay Transparency Directive) |
| **People Analytics Director** | Methodology rigor, model validation, data governance, HRIS integration |
| **HR Technology Analyst** | Is this software or advisory? Security posture, scalability, roadmap, RFP-readiness |
| **Gartner Analyst** | Market category, defensible differentiation, evidence behind claims, where this sits vs. named leaders |
| **Enterprise Sales Director** | Deal-closability: proof points, procurement collateral, references, multi-stakeholder buy-in |

Every "Would I buy?" answer is a genuine verdict, not encouragement. Where the honest answer is "no, not yet," the rewrite section shows what closes the gap.

---

## Executive Verdict (TL;DR)

As it stands today, datalentsolutions.com reads as a **credible, well-intentioned SME-focused HR boutique** with generic AI language ("IA generativa," "automatización," "analítica predictiva"). It does **not** currently position Datalent Solutions as a European leader in Agentic AI for HR — the phrase "agentic" never appears, there is no named AI product, no autonomous-workflow proof, and no EU AI Act / data-sovereignty narrative, which is the single most defensible wedge a European HR-AI company has against U.S. platforms right now. Against Mercer, Korn Ferry, Visier, Deloitte, and Workday, the site currently competes on tone, not on evidence — and tone loses every enterprise deal.

The good news: the underlying service portfolio (predictive recruitment, org diagnostics, upskilling, AI automation) is a reasonable foundation, and "boutique + European + human-governed AI" is a real, ownable position that none of the five named competitors occupy cleanly. The rewrite sections below build toward that position without fabricating scale Datalent doesn't have.

### Consolidated Objections
1. **No proof.** 10 clients, 65% time-to-hire reduction, 92% first-year retention — no source, no date, no methodology. Combined with first-name-only testimonials, every number reads as marketing rather than evidence.
2. **No comp/total-rewards line.** A glaring gap given regulatory timing (EU Pay Transparency Directive) and given that Mercer and Korn Ferry both lead with comp as a core pillar.
3. **AI claims are generic, not agentic.** "IA generativa" and "automatización" describe assistive tools, not autonomous agents taking multi-step action (screening → scheduling → drafting an offer → escalating exceptions to a human). That distinction is exactly what "agentic AI" means to a 2026 buyer, and the page doesn't make it.
4. **No security/trust/data-governance surface.** For a company processing candidate and employee data (a GDPR special-attention category via inference), there is no visible statement on data residency, model governance, bias auditing, or EU AI Act risk classification.
5. **No enterprise proof or scale signal.** No named logos, no employee count, no funding/backing signal, no partner integrations.
6. **Ambiguous business model.** Software, service, or hybrid — unclear, which stalls any serious buyer's evaluation process before it starts.

### What's Missing (by persona)
- **CHRO:** a downloadable case study or one-pager with a named (even anonymized-but-detailed) enterprise outcome.
- **Compensation Director:** a fifth service line — pay equity audit / total rewards benchmarking — or an explicit statement of why it's intentionally out of scope.
- **People Analytics Director:** a "How our models work" methodology section — data sources, validation approach, bias testing, explainability.
- **HR Technology Analyst:** an integrations/architecture section, and a security & compliance page (SOC2/ISO 27001 status or roadmap, GDPR posture, data residency).
- **Gartner Analyst:** a named, defined AI product (not just adjectives), a whitepaper or research artifact, and any third-party validation (award, analyst mention, press).
- **Enterprise Sales Director:** case studies with real (or realistically anonymized) logos, a pricing/engagement-model page, and a clear "Request enterprise briefing" path distinct from the SMB "free diagnostic" CTA.

### Q5 — Does this page position Datalent as a European leader in Agentic AI for HR?
**No.** Three concrete reasons:
1. The word "agentic" is absent. Without naming the category, Datalent cannot lead it — buyers and analysts search for and compare against category terms, not adjectives.
2. There is no autonomous-workflow proof. "Automatización de procesos de reclutamiento" and "chatbots internos" describe *assisted* automation (a human still triggers and reviews each step), not *agentic* automation (an agent plans and executes a multi-step workflow with defined autonomy and human-in-the-loop exception handling). The distinction is the entire point of the category.
3. There is no European-specific angle. "European leader" requires a reason a European buyer should prefer Datalent over Workday/Visier specifically *because* it's European — data residency, EU AI Act risk-tiering support, GDPR-native model governance. None of this appears. Right now "European" describes Datalent's office address, not its product thesis.

## Cross-Page Strategic Recommendations

To honestly earn the claim "European leader in Agentic AI for HR" — not just state it — five things need to happen above and beyond page copy:

1. **Name and define the agentic AI product line.** A category claim needs a proper noun. Whatever the internal name becomes, it needs one consistent definition used identically on Home, in the newsletter format, and in the new FAQ block.
2. **Publish an AI governance/trust page** covering autonomy limits, human-in-the-loop checkpoints, data residency, and EU AI Act risk classification. This single page does more competitive work against Workday specifically than any other content investment, because it's the one dimension where "European, boutique, human-governed" beats "American, platform-scale, agent-everywhere."
3. **Decide, explicitly, whether compensation/pay-equity is in scope.** Given the EU Pay Transparency Directive's transposition deadline has already passed, silence on comp reads as a gap to every Compensation Director and increasingly to every CHRO. Either add the service line or state the deliberate non-focus — both beat the current silence.
4. **Turn the newsletter into a citable research artifact**, not commentary — this is the cheapest path to the kind of analyst-referenceable content Deloitte and Mercer use to anchor their category leadership.
5. **Build one enterprise-grade case study**, even anonymized ("a 400-person Spanish manufacturing group"), with a named methodology and a dated, sourced outcome. One real, defensible case study outweighs all three unsourced hero stats combined, and unlocks the Enterprise Sales Director's entire evaluation process.

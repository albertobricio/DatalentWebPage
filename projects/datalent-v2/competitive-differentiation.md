# Competitive Differentiation — Datalent Solutions

**Objective this document serves:** turn `.claude/memory/competitive-landscape.md`'s descriptive comparison into five actionable battle plans — how Datalent actually wins (or knowingly loses) a competitive deal against each named player. Structured per `.claude/templates/competitive-battlecard-template.md`'s existing shape, condensed to five entries here.

**Evidence basis:** every factual claim about a competitor is a general, publicly-known structural fact (headquarters location, product category, market approach) — no invented statistic about a competitor's revenue, client count, or market share appears anywhere below, consistent with `.claude/memory/non-fabrication-policy.md` applied to competitive claims, not just self-claims.

## The Shared Wedge

Before the five individual plans: every battle plan below draws on the same underlying wedge, so sales conversations stay consistent (`go-to-market.md`'s sales enablement depends on this). The wedge is `positioning.md`'s Pillars 2 and 3 combined: **no competitor in this set combines European-native AI governance with senior, boutique-scale judgment.** Each battlecard below is a variation on making that gap concrete for a specific competitor's specific weakness.

## vs. Workday

**Their position:** US-headquartered HCM system of record, the platform most of Tier 2/3's prospects already run, and — as of 2025–2026 — the loudest public marketer of "agentic AI" in HR specifically (their Illuminate/agent features).
**Where they're genuinely stronger:** platform scale, existing data integration, an enormous partner ecosystem, and incumbency — displacing Workday as the system of record is not a fight Datalent should pick.
**Where Datalent is genuinely stronger:** independent governance credibility. A platform vendor cannot credibly self-certify its own AI feature's compliance to a skeptical internal Legal or works-council audience — that's a structural conflict of interest Datalent doesn't have.
**The actual play:** never position as a Workday replacement. Position as the **independent governance layer for a Workday deployment** — audit, validate, or co-design the specific agentic feature the client is piloting. This is `ideal-client-profiles.md` Tier 3's exact entry point.
**Landmine:** never claim Datalent's agents are "as capable as" Workday's at platform scale — that's an unwinnable, and unnecessary, comparison. The claim is governance depth, not feature parity.

## vs. Visier

**Their position:** a people analytics SaaS platform with an embedded AI assistant, Gartner-recognized, strong data-model and benchmarking infrastructure.
**Where they're genuinely stronger:** breadth of pre-built analytics infrastructure and benchmark data assembled at platform scale — Datalent cannot out-build this with a boutique team.
**Where Datalent is genuinely stronger:** methodology transparency and governance. Visier's value is largely in its data model and dashboards; it is not positioned as an AI *governance* partner, and its analytics, however sophisticated, don't carry the explicit bias-audit and human-oversight design `.claude/agents/ai-governance-auditor.md` builds into every deliverable.
**The actual play:** for a client already running Visier, position Datalent as the governance and methodology-validation layer on top of it (does this model's output survive a bias audit? is it EU AI Act risk-classified?) rather than a replacement platform. For a client not yet on any platform, compete directly on "methodology you can see, not just a dashboard you trust."
**Landmine:** don't compete on data-visualization polish — Datalent will lose that comparison and it's not the actual differentiator.

## vs. Mercer

**Their position:** the largest, most established global HR/comp consulting brand, decades of proprietary benchmarking data, deep compensation and total rewards heritage.
**Where they're genuinely stronger:** scale, brand trust, and the sheer depth of proprietary market data behind their compensation benchmarking — this is a real, hard-to-replicate asset.
**Where Datalent is genuinely stronger:** speed, senior-only delivery (no large junior-analyst pools diluting quality), and — critically — an actual defined agentic AI capability, which Mercer's AI-augmented-advisory positioning doesn't match.
**The actual play:** for Tier 1/2 clients, compete on responsiveness and senior attention Mercer's scale structurally can't offer at smaller deal sizes. For any AI Governance or Agentic AI engagement specifically, compete on having a real product category Mercer doesn't — Mercer sells advisory informed by AI, not agentic systems designed and governed as a discipline.
**Landmine:** never compete head-on on raw compensation benchmarking data breadth — Mercer's proprietary survey depth is a genuine, currently unmatchable asset; win on methodology transparency and speed instead.

## vs. Korn Ferry

**Their position:** global executive search and leadership assessment brand, with a proprietary assessment framework (KF4D) and a digital advisory arm.
**Where they're genuinely stronger:** search and senior-leadership assessment reputation — a category Datalent doesn't compete in at all.
**Where Datalent is genuinely stronger:** the AI governance and agentic AI categories are simply not where Korn Ferry plays; there's limited direct overlap outside People Analytics and Recruitment Predictivo.
**The actual play:** rarely a head-to-head competitive deal — more often a coexistence scenario (a client using Korn Ferry for executive search and Datalent for AI-governed operational hiring and analytics). Where overlap does occur, win on the AI/governance dimension Korn Ferry doesn't own.
**Landmine:** don't position against Korn Ferry as if it's a like-for-like competitor in most deals — misreading this as a head-to-head fight wastes positioning energy on a comparison prospects aren't actually making.

## vs. Deloitte

**Their position:** Big Four consulting scale, an annual, widely-cited Human Capital Trends research program, a large enterprise GenAI advisory practice.
**Where they're genuinely stronger:** brand trust at the largest enterprise scale, and a thought-leadership research operation Datalent cannot match in volume.
**Where Datalent is genuinely stronger:** speed, cost (a Big Four engagement carries Big Four overhead and pricing), and — again — a genuinely defined, narrow agentic AI product rather than broad GenAI advisory commentary.
**The actual play:** for Tier 1/2, compete on being fast and affordable where Deloitte is neither. For AI Governance specifically, compete on depth-per-euro — a focused governance engagement from a specialist beats a slice of a generalist Big Four AI advisory practice's attention.
**Landmine:** don't try to out-publish Deloitte's Human Capital Trends volume; out-cite them instead, per `roadmap.md`'s newsletter program — fewer, better-sourced pieces beat more, thinner ones.
